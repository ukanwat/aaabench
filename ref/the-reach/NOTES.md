## What is actually there — observations

- **Ground:** Residential streets are asphalt, mid-grey and slightly coarse, with **no centre line at all** — the marking on a Reach street is a stop bar and nothing else. There is a **granite or concrete kerb with a 150 mm face, broken every 15–20 m by a wide dropped driveway apron**, so the kerb line is a dashed thing rather than a continuous one. A grass verge 1.2–2.0 m wide separates the kerb from a **buff-coloured concrete footway** in 1.2 m saw-cut panels, and the verge is where the hydrants, poles and street trees live. Kerb inlet gullies sit at the low points with a cast lintel and a dark slot. The arterials are the opposite: white edge lines, white lane dashes, a solid yellow against a thin raised concrete median, weeds along the median joint, and long black tyre smears at every signal.
- **Facades and ground floor:** Two families. Housing: 1½ or 2 storeys, **horizontal vinyl or aluminium siding in blue-grey, white, cream or tan**, asphalt shingle roof in tan/brown/grey, a shed or gable dormer, white-framed double-hung windows with applied grille bars, a small pedimented porch with white turned posts and rails, and a **tall red brick chimney on the gable end**. Very often an attached garage or carport with the drive running past the house. Retail: a single-storey strip mall, 4–6 units, with a continuous **green standing-seam mansard fascia**, a fabric awning below it, plate glass to the ground, and fake random-ashlar stone veneer on the piers.
- **Roofline and clutter:** Nothing above two storeys anywhere except the water tower. Roofs are simple gables and hips in asphalt shingle with a ridge vent and a couple of black plastic soil vents. Satellite dishes on the gable end at eaves level. Small aluminium ridge/turbine vents. On the strip mall, the mansard hides all the plant, and there are two decorative towers with pyramidal caps to break up the run.
- **Verticality — poles, cables, signs:** Timber poles at 35–45 m along one side of every street, with **grey pole-mounted transformer cans on the arterial poles**, and wires crossing junctions diagonally in fans. Street lighting is a small cobra head on a bracket off the same timber pole, not its own column. Traffic signals only at the arterial junctions, on long mast arms. Residential junctions are all-way STOP with the sign on a plain grey U-channel post. Sign clutter is low. The two things that stand above everything: the **water tower**, and the **tall pole lights over a strip-mall car park**.
- **Materials and their ageing:** Vinyl siding chalks and slightly ripples; asphalt shingle fades and grows dark algae streaks running down from the ridge; white PVC fencing goes slightly grey-green at the base; brick chimneys get a white efflorescence bloom near the top; the concrete footway crazes and settles at the driveway crossings and is lifted by tree roots. Asphalt is patched at every utility cut and the patches are a different, blacker grey.
- **Colour:** Green — a lot of it, and mown. Then the siding palette: **slate blue, white, cream, tan, sage**, with white trim, and roofs in tan-brown or charcoal. Red brick chimneys. Buff concrete footways. The strip mall's **dark green metal and maroon awning** with cream stone veneer. Yellow hydrants. And, at night, saturated signage: red, white, yellow, and the white-blue of the car-park pole lights.
- **Light:** 40.7 °N and completely flat, with a tree canopy over the residential streets and none at all on the arterials. So: dappled shifting light on the side streets, hard open light on the arterials. In the November images the sun is very low and throws 25 m shadows straight down the road. The important state to build is the wet dusk one: **the road is dark, the sky is a deep blue-grey, and the only light is the strip-mall signage and the car-park poles reflected as long vertical smears in the wet asphalt**.
- **Vegetation:** Heavy and municipal. **London plane / sycamore street trees in the verge, pollarded so their crowns are knuckled and stubby** — very distinctive in winter. In the gardens: clipped juniper and arborvitae globes, cones and spirals; a crape myrtle or ornamental cherry; foundation planting in a mulched bed with a brick or plastic edge; and lawn everywhere, mown to the kerb. Nothing is wild.
- **People and vehicles:** No pedestrians in any frame. Vehicles everywhere: minivans, SUVs, sedans and pickups **parked at the kerb even though every house has a driveway**, plus a flatbed truck and a bus on the arterial. On the arterial at dusk the traffic is continuous. Dustbins at the kerb.
- **The tells:**
  - The kerb broken by a driveway apron every 15–20 m, so it is never continuous.
  - Street trees pollarded into knuckled stubs to keep them off the wires.
  - Clipped juniper topiary and a mulch bed with a brick edge in front of every house.
  - A white vinyl picket fence running the whole frontage and stopping dead at the property line.
  - Two grey transformer cans on one pole and none on the next four.
  - Green plastic mesh site fencing along a whole block.
  - A utility pole wrapped in artificial flowers.
  - Dustbins standing at the kerb on a random weekday.
  - Weeds in the joint between the median kerb and the asphalt.
  - Dark algae streaks running down an asphalt-shingle roof from the ridge.
  - The strip mall's two decorative towers, which do nothing.
  - Cars parked at the kerb in front of empty driveways.

**Where the photographs contradicted what I would have assumed:**
1. There is **no centre line** on the residential streets. None at all. The road is a plain grey strip and the only paint is a stop bar.
2. The street trees are **pollarded into knuckled stubs** to keep them clear of the overhead wires. That single silhouette is more characteristic of postwar suburbia than any house detail.
3. Nobody uses their driveway. Cars are at the kerb.
4. The houses are not identical any more. Seventy years of alterations means the row is blue siding / white Cape / brick ranch / grey two-storey, all on the same footprint and setback. **The uniformity is in the setback and the plot, not the building.**
5. The median on the arterial is a **thin weedy concrete kerb strip**, not a planted island.

## Numbers I could measure or infer
Estimates, scaled off cars, hydrants (0.75 m) and doors.

- **Residential street:** **9.0–10.0 m kerb to kerb**, no centre line, kerbside parking on both sides without marked bays.
- **Verge:** 1.2–2.0 m; **footway 1.2–1.5 m** in 1.2 m concrete panels; total kerb to boundary 3.0–3.5 m.
- **Front setback:** **8–11 m** from the boundary to the house face, all of it lawn plus a driveway.
- **Plot:** roughly **18–20 m frontage × 30–35 m deep** (≈ 0.06 ha), houses 4–6 m apart at the side.
- **House:** footprint 9–11 m wide × 8–10 m deep; ground floor 2.5 m floor to ceiling, eaves at 3.0–5.5 m, ridge at **6.5–8.0 m**; roof pitch 35–45°; gable-end brick chimney 0.8 × 0.6 m, 1.0 m above the ridge.
- **Driveway:** 3.0–3.6 m wide, apron 4.5–5.5 m at the kerb, dropped-kerb crossings every 15–20 m.
- **Arterial:** 2 lanes each way at 3.5 m plus a **1.2–1.8 m raised concrete median** = 16–18 m kerb to kerb; signal junctions every 300–500 m.
- **Utility pole:** 10–11 m, spaced 35–45 m; transformer cans at ~7.5 m; lowest conductor ~6.5 m; the cobra streetlight bracket at 8 m with a 1.5 m arm.
- **Street tree:** pollarded plane, 8–12 m tall, spaced 12–18 m, trunk 400–600 mm.
- **Strip mall:** unit frontage **6.0–7.5 m**; fascia band top at 5.0–5.5 m; awning underside 2.4 m projecting 1.5 m; car park 18–22 m deep in front with 90° bays; pole lights 9–11 m at 25 m spacing.
- **Water tower:** bowl ~14 m diameter, overall **35–42 m** — roughly 3× the tree canopy, and the only landmark.
