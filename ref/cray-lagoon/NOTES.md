# cray-lagoon — reference board

Anchors: Barnegat Bay, New Jersey, (39.7500, −74.1800) · Chincoteague Island, Virginia, (37.9330, −75.3790)

Coverage note: **Barnegat Bay has no Mapillary coverage** at the anchor (the coordinate is in open water — the nearest land is Island Beach State Park). Its geosearch results are all wide water-and-sky panoramas, which turn out to be exactly what the district needs. Chincoteague has good Mapillary (Jan 2021, Jun 2022) covering the causeway, the low bridges and Main Street. Three images — the crab shanties, the boardwalk/hide and the salt-meadow close-ups — are keyword-sourced from Tangier Island and other Atlantic marshes and are labelled as such.

## What is actually there — observations

- **Ground:** Three surfaces and a lot of water. (1) The **causeway** — asphalt, double yellow, white edge lines, and a galvanised W-beam guardrail on both sides for its entire length, sitting on an embankment that is only **1–2 m above the marsh**; the shoulder is gravel and the marsh starts immediately. (2) The **low bridges** — pale concrete deck, transverse joints, an open steel rail on splayed galvanised posts. (3) The **one street** — asphalt with a **yellow-painted kerb**, a concrete footway, and a bike stencil. Everything else is either marsh mat, open water, or timber decking. There is no kerb, drain or footway anywhere off that single street.
- **Facades and ground floor:** Nothing over two storeys and almost nothing over one. The building types are: a **timber shed on piles** (gable, board or panel clad, one door, corrugated or asphalt roof); a **painted concrete-block commercial box** (flat roof, black box awnings, a hanging sign on a timber bracket, roses at the base); a **grey or white clapboard cottage** with a glazed bay and a shingle roof; and one **1930s red brick civic block** with cream bands and arched engine doors. Signage is a hanging board on a bracket or hand-painted lettering on a timber frame stuck in the grass.
- **Roofline and clutter:** Simple gables and flats. What sits above the roofline is not architecture — it is **timber utility poles, transformer cans, and a fan of eight or nine wires**, plus a small municipal water tower on a stalk and, crossing the lagoon, a **line of lattice transmission towers**. The visual clutter at ground level is the gear: **stacks of wire crab pots two and three high**, floats, coiled rope, outboards, and boat trailers.
- **Verticality — poles, cables, signs:** The single most characteristic vertical is a **timber pile** — as a bearing pile under a shanty, as a dolphin in open water, and, most tellingly, as a **broken stub standing alone in the water where something used to be**. After that, timber utility poles carrying a single wire *out across open marsh to a shed*, and in the settlement a pole carrying two transformer cans and a sky full of wire. Then the pylon line. Signs are low, hand-painted, and mounted on timber frames in the grass.
- **Materials and their ageing:** Timber goes **silver-grey** everywhere it is not painted, and the piles rot from the waterline up so they are fatter at the bottom and pointed at the top. Paint on the shanties is thin, chalky and rarely matches its neighbour. Galvanised steel — guardrail, bridge rail, boardwalk railing, crab pot wire — goes chalk-white with rust blooming at every weld and cut. Concrete block picks up a green-black algae line at the base. Everything metal is salt-hazed.
- **Colour:** In autumn and winter the marsh mat is the whole palette: **old gold, cinnamon, rust-orange, fawn and olive**, laid flat and swirled. In summer it is a uniform mid-green with the same texture. Water is **pale grey-green under cloud, silver under overcast, and a full sky-mirror of pink, lilac and orange at dawn**. Add silver-grey timber, chalk-grey galvanised steel, and the small painted notes: butter yellow, dull red, white, a mustard-ochre hide, a red flag. The sky is more than half of every frame.
- **Light:** 38–40 °N, completely open, and the water doubles everything. There is no shadow of any kind cast by the landscape, so the whole district is lit by sky and by **bounce off the water surface**, which fills in every underside. At dawn and dusk the lagoon becomes a mirror and the sky is reproduced upside down beneath the horizon with only a slight loss of contrast — that doubling is the district's signature image. Under overcast, water and sky come within a stop of each other and the horizon nearly disappears. There is **no street lighting at all** off the one street.
- **Vegetation:** Two grasses, and the difference matters. **Salt-meadow cordgrass** on the high marsh — fine, 0.3–0.6 m, and **laid flat by the tide into big whorls and cowlicks** that stay all winter. **Common reed** on the disturbed edges and along the causeway — 2.5–4 m, standing, with plumes. Plus low salt-burnt shrub (bayberry, groundsel bush) on the slightly higher ground, and a fringe of loblolly/pitch pine and hardwood only where there is real elevation. Ornamental planting exists in exactly one place: **shrub roses in a mulch bed against a restaurant wall**.
- **People and vehicles:** Almost nobody. One pedestrian in eleven frames. Vehicles: pickups, SUVs, a boat on a trailer. The real population is birds — **herring gulls perched on bridge rails at arm's length from passing traffic**, plus waders and terns out on the flats. And working boats: a white deadrise workboat, small skiffs, an outboard tipped up.
- **The tells:**
  - Broken timber pile stubs standing in open water with nothing on them.
  - A single wire running from a pole out across open marsh to one shed.
  - Stacks of wire crab pots, two and three high, covering a shanty deck.
  - Four shanties in a row, all different colours, none matching.
  - Gulls standing on the bridge rail while cars pass a metre away.
  - Guardrail with bright beam and rusted posts, running for a kilometre.
  - A yellow-painted kerb, and a white-painted plinth at the base of a brick building.
  - Hand-painted signage on a timber frame stuck in the grass.
  - A pylon line crossing the water and reflecting in it.
  - Oyster/clam racks as low dark lines in the shallows.
  - Cowlick whorls in the laid-flat marsh grass.
  - A small blue water tower on the far bank as the only vertical in the settlement.

**Where the photographs contradicted what I would have assumed:**
1. **Salt marsh does not stand up.** The high marsh is a *laid mat*, swirled into whorls by the tide, and it stays that way all winter. I would have modelled it as upright grass. The whorl pattern is the single most valuable texture note on this board.
2. There are **two different grasses** with completely different heights and behaviours — the 0.4 m laid meadow and the 3 m standing reed — and where they meet is a hard edge, not a gradient. MAP_PLAN says "phragmites to the horizon"; the truthful version is *reed on the disturbed edges, laid cordgrass meadow over the bulk of it.*
3. The lagoon at dawn is a **perfect mirror**, and the far shore is a single dark line only a few pixels tall. Almost the whole frame is sky and reflected sky.
4. Gulls perch on the bridge rail, at arm's length from traffic, in numbers. Birds here are not distant wildlife; they are street furniture.
5. The kerbs in the settlement are **painted yellow**, and the brick building has a **white-painted plinth** — both flood/visibility details I would never have added.

## Numbers I could measure or infer
Estimates, scaled off cars, guardrail (0.75 m to the beam), gulls (~0.6 m long) and doors.

- **Causeway:** carriageway **7.0–7.5 m** (two 3.5 m lanes), gravel shoulder 0.8–1.2 m, guardrail 0.75 m high with posts at 1.9 m, embankment top **1.0–2.0 m above the marsh**, side slopes about 1:2 with riprap at the toe.
- **Low bridge:** deck **8–9 m** wide including a 0.5 m raised safety kerb; parapet rail 1.05 m high on splayed posts at 2.4 m; deck **1.5–2.5 m above high water**; spans 12–18 m on pile bents.
- **Settlement street:** **9–10 m** kerb to kerb, footway 1.5 m on one side, kerb painted yellow.
- **Crab shanty:** **3.5–5.0 m wide × 4.5–6.0 m deep**, eaves at 2.2–2.5 m, ridge at 3.5–4.0 m; deck 0.9–1.5 m above high water; **piles 250–350 mm dia at 1.8–2.4 m centres**, standing 1.5–2.5 m proud; walkway 1.2 m wide.
- **Crab pot:** 0.6 m cube, stacked 2–3 high.
- **Boardwalk:** deck **1.5–1.8 m wide**, 0.4–0.8 m above the marsh, planks 140 mm, posts at 2.4 m with **four horizontal wire strands** at 250 mm spacing.
- **Bird hide:** roughly **3.0 × 4.0 m**, eaves 2.1 m, ridge 3.2 m; viewing slot 0.35 m tall at 1.25 m above the deck, running most of the wall.
- **Salt-meadow cordgrass:** **0.3–0.6 m** when standing, laid flat to 0.1–0.2 m; whorls **1.0–2.5 m across**. Common reed on the edges: **2.5–4.0 m**.
- **Creek:** 3–10 m wide with vertical peat banks 0.4–0.8 m at low water, meandering with a wavelength of 30–60 m.
- **Utility pole:** 9–10 m, spaced 40–50 m, often carrying a single conductor out over the marsh.
- **Transmission tower over the lagoon:** 30–45 m, spans 250–350 m.
- **Commercial block:** single storey, parapet at **4.0–4.5 m**; awning underside 2.3 m projecting 1.0 m; hanging sign 1.2 × 1.6 m at 2.6 m to the underside.
- **Horizon:** in the lagoon panoramas the far shore occupies **under 3 % of the image height**. That ratio is the district.
