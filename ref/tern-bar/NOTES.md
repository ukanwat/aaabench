# tern-bar — reference board

Anchors: Wildwood / Wildwood Crest, New Jersey, (38.9890, −74.8150) · Provincetown, Cape Cod, Massachusetts, (42.0500, −70.1870)

Coverage note: Wildwood has one good Mapillary sequence (September 2015) covering Rio Grande Ave and the cross avenues at driver eye height, plus recent (Nov 2025) Wikimedia street photography. **Provincetown has no Mapillary coverage** and its geosearch results are mostly night shots of the harbour, which were unusable — the one Provincetown image I kept is a daytime facade study. The dune-fence and pitch-pine images are keyword-sourced, from Ocean City MD and a pine-barren dune respectively, and are labelled as such.

## What is actually there — observations

- **Ground:** The strip is asphalt, and it is a **warm pinkish-grey**, coarse-aggregate and sun-bleached, not the blue-black of a city — that surprised me. It's patched but not badly, and it carries a **double yellow centre line** with white lane dashes, plus long black tyre-rubber smears from stopping traffic. Junctions in the improved sections are paved wall to wall in **red-buff brick with a blue-and-sand wave mosaic** in the middle. Kerbs are concrete, low, with generous concrete sidewalks (1.8–2.5 m) that are set back behind a grass verge on the residential streets. The boardwalk is a completely different ground: **bleached silver-grey timber planks in alternating diagonal panels**, each panel about 3 m square, with dark gaps and sand blown into them. Beach sand is pale orange-buff, rippled, criss-crossed with tyre tracks from the grooming machine and footprints.
- **Facades and ground floor:** Almost everything is one or two storeys. Three types in the same street: (1) mid-century motel — white or cream stucco/block, horizontal trim bands in mint or turquoise, an external first-floor walkway with a wrought-iron balustrade, doors painted turquoise, a cantilevered carport; (2) clapboard/shingle cottage — 1½ storeys, gabled, white vinyl picket fence, front porch; (3) low commercial box — flat roof, brick or stucco, a metal fascia with hand-painted or vinyl lettering, a canvas awning. The boardwalk stalls are the fourth: **entirely open-fronted under a green steel canopy**, with merchandise hung on every surface and a roller shutter to close.
- **Roofline and clutter:** Nothing tall. What you get above eaves level: a **white water tower on a slender stalk** which is the single navigational landmark; the freestanding motel pylon signs, which are *taller than the motels*; satellite dishes and black flues on the boardwalk stall roofs; window and packaged AC units; and the fairground rides at one end of the boardwalk. Roofs are asphalt shingle in a mid-brown or blue-grey, or flat with a gravel finish.
- **Verticality — poles, cables, signs:** Heavy overhead wiring on **timber poles at 35–45 m**, with drops to every building, so the sky is crossed by wires at every angle. Traffic signals are on **long galvanised mast arms reaching a third to half way across the road**, sometimes with a second signal head hanging from a catenary. Floodlights and CCTV cameras are strapped to the same poles. Commercial signs are bolted to *those poles too*. Streetlights: a plain grey cobra head on the arterial; on the boardwalk, **bright blue painted columns with a curved arm**. Then the big one — the **freestanding pylon sign** at the kerb, 3–5 m, neon-outlined, shaped like an object, with a VACANCY panel.
- **Materials and their ageing:** Painted stucco and block chalking and going dusty. Vinyl and aluminium siding chalking too, and slightly rippled where it has expanded. Timber going **silver-grey everywhere it is not painted** — boardwalk, dune fence, posts. White paint on trim always the freshest thing and always slightly blistered at the south face. Salt haze on everything metal, and rust starting at every fixing, screw head and cut edge. Neon tube supports and their transformer boxes rusting on the back of the sign.
- **Colour:** This is a strongly coloured district and the palette is specific: **mint green, turquoise, and white** on the motels; **hot pink, red and yellow** on the boardwalk signage; **bright blue** on the lamp columns; **pale orange-buff** sand and **silver-grey** timber; **lilac, sage, sea-green and butter-yellow clapboard with brilliant white trim** on the Cape Cod houses; and a sky that is a deep saturated blue in summer because there is no pollution and a lot of water. The asphalt is pinkish-grey. Out of season everything drops to grey-brown grass, bare trees and the same white trim.
- **Light:** 39 °N, flat land, nothing to block anything. Summer noon is nearly shadowless and blows out the white walls; the whole place is high-key. Because it is on a spit, **light bounces off water on both sides and off the sand**, so undersides — carport soffits, balcony ceilings, the underside of the boardwalk canopy — are lit almost as brightly as the top surfaces. That is the district's distinguishing lighting fact. Low sun in the morning and evening comes straight down the east–west cross streets. Off season, the low November sun throws 20 m shadows across an empty road. At night the boardwalk is the only lit thing for a kilometre and it is lit by **neon and by the signs themselves**, not by streetlights.
- **Vegetation:** On the streets: large spreading maples and a few ornamental pears, mown grass verges, clipped yew and juniper against the houses. On the dune: **marram/beach grass in clumps with sand between**, low bayberry and beach plum scrub, and **pitch pine that is leggy, twisted, mostly dead branch stub, with all the green in a thin flat crown at the top**. There is no continuous canopy — the pines stand apart with sand between them.
- **People and vehicles:** In summer on the boardwalk, dispersed — maybe 40 people over 200 m, mostly in twos and threes, walking slowly, several with pushchairs, nobody in a hurry, and everyone is dressed for the beach whether they are on it or not. On the strip, almost nobody on foot. Vehicles: SUVs, pickups, and a lot of older cars; a yellow tram train on the boardwalk; bicycles on the boardwalk in the morning. Parking is angled or parallel on the strip and half-on-the-verge in the back streets.
- **The tells:**
  - The sign is taller than the building it advertises.
  - A restaurant's sign bolted to the municipal traffic-signal pole.
  - A CCTV camera and a floodlight strapped to a wooden power pole.
  - Coloured brick paving with a wave mosaic across a junction.
  - Yellow-painted timber bollards along a kerb, several leaning.
  - The dune fence half buried, its slats at inconsistent angles.
  - A rope swagged between square posts in front of the dune fence.
  - Boardwalk planks laid in alternating diagonal panels, silver-grey.
  - Merchandise hanging off every surface of an open-fronted stall.
  - A red vending machine outside a motel office.
  - Turquoise metal patio chairs stacked by a pool.
  - A roller shutter pulled down on a boardwalk stall in the off season.
  - Bicycle racks and a bike lane that only exist for two months a year.

**Where the photographs contradicted what I would have assumed:**
1. The asphalt is **warm pinkish-grey**, not black. Sun and salt bleach it, and this changes the entire value key of the district — Tern Bar should read *light* underfoot.
2. The boardwalk is **diagonal herringbone panels**, not straight parallel planks. Straight planks would look immediately wrong.
3. The motel palette is **mint green and turquoise on white**, and it is applied as thin horizontal bands and door colour, not as a whole-building colour. The buildings are mostly white.
4. There is a **water tower on a stalk**, and it is the only tall thing. In a district where everything is one or two storeys, that is the navigation landmark and it needs to be in the plan.
5. Out of season the district is **completely empty** — the November photograph has zero people and two moving cars. That, not the summer crowd, is the default state and is far more interesting to walk through.

## Numbers I could measure or infer
Estimates, scaled off cars, doors and people.

- **Strip arterial:** 4 lanes at 3.5 m plus parking = **17–20 m kerb to kerb**; sidewalk 2.0–2.5 m each side.
- **Residential cross street:** 10–12 m kerb to kerb, sidewalk 1.5 m set behind a 1.5–2.0 m grass verge.
- **Motel:** two storeys, floor to floor **2.8–3.0 m** (low — these are cheap buildings), parapet/fascia at ~7 m; unit width **3.6–4.2 m**; balcony walkway 1.5 m deep with a 1.05 m balustrade; carport slab clear height 2.7 m projecting 5–6 m.
- **Pylon sign:** overall height **4.0–5.5 m**, sign panel 2.5 × 2.5 m, set 0.5–1.0 m back from the kerb.
- **Boardwalk:** **12–15 m wide** — much wider than expected, wide enough for a tram and two lanes of pedestrians; deck 1.5–2.5 m above the sand; plank 140 mm wide, panels ~3 m square in alternating diagonals.
- **Boardwalk stall:** open frontage 4–6 m wide, canopy underside at 3.0–3.5 m.
- **Water tower:** bowl ~10 m diameter, overall **35–45 m** to the top — about six times the height of anything else.
- **Dune fence:** slats 1.2 m tall × 40 mm wide at ~60 mm spacing, wired in three rows, posts at 3.0 m; sand drifted 0.3–0.6 m up the windward face.
- **Rope line:** posts 100 mm square, 0.9 m above sand, at 4–5 m spacing, rope sagging 0.25 m at mid-span.
- **Pitch pine:** 6–10 m tall, clear bare trunk for the lower 60–70 %, crown 3–4 m across and only 1.5–2 m deep, spaced 8–15 m apart with open sand between.
- **Utility poles:** 10–11 m, 35–45 m spacing; signal mast arms reaching **6–9 m** out over the carriageway.
- **Front setback:** motels 6–10 m (the pool and the parking sit in it); cottages 4–6 m.
