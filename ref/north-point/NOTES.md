# north-point — reference board

Anchors as given in MAP_PLAN: (37.8590, −122.4850) · (41.4600, −71.3400)

**Anchor problem.** (37.8590, −122.4850) is **Wolf Ridge / Fort Cronkhite**, in the northern Marin Headlands. Geosearch there returns nothing but HAER survey photographs of 1940s anti-aircraft gun batteries and army storehouses — no switchbacks, no view of the city, no estates. The switchback pleasure road that MAP_PLAN is describing (Vantage Drive) is **Conzelman Road, ≈ (37.8280, −122.4900)**, 3.5 km south, and Point Bonita Lighthouse is at **≈ (37.8158, −122.5295)**. I gathered from those two points instead. **Recommend correcting the anchor to (37.8280, −122.4900).**

**Coverage.** Mapillary coverage of the Marin Headlands is **thin but real**, and only findable with a small bounding box — the API rejects a box larger than roughly 0.02° with "Please reduce the amount of data you're asking for", which is easy to mistake for "no coverage". Conzelman Road has usable sequences at (37.8295, −122.4846) and (37.8312, −122.4799), and Point Bonita has two 2011 frames. Newport has good May-2016 dashcam coverage of Ocean Drive and Bellevue Avenue. **KartaView returns `n = 0` at both anchors.**

Anchors actually used: Conzelman Rd & Point Bonita, Marin Headlands, CA, (37.8280, −122.4900) / (37.8158, −122.5295) · Ocean Drive & Bellevue Ave, Newport RI, (41.4600, −71.3400)

## What is actually there — observations

- **The switchback road itself (image 14):** two lanes, no footway, cut into the hillside so there is a **raw rock cut on the inside and a fall on the outside**. On the uphill side there is a **yellow-painted concrete kerb-and-channel** — a continuous drainage line, not a normal kerb — with short yellow-topped marker posts at the tightest points. The centre line is a double yellow, and where the road narrows the taper is painted as a **chevron**, not a simple merge. The outside shoulder is **used as parking, nose to tail, for hundreds of metres**, with people walking in the gap between the parked cars and the traffic — which is the single most useful thing on this board for making Vantage Drive feel used rather than empty. The view is on **one side only** and it appears suddenly as the road crests a spur.
- **Ground:** Estate roads are asphalt, dark and coarse-aggregate, with **no kerb on the wall side and no footway at all** — the carriageway meets a mown grass verge which meets the wall. Where there is a kerb it is granite, on the sea side only. Markings are a **double yellow centre line, a white edge line, and occasionally a bike stencil**, and the surface is crack-sealed in long black tar snakes rather than patched in slabs. Edging is often **loose granite boulders or whitewashed rocks laid in the grass** rather than a kerb — a completely different vocabulary from the city. At the water the ground goes to a concrete or gravel pull-off, then angular riprap with grass growing between the blocks. At the lighthouse and on the headland the ground is bare stone, gravel path, and dry grass.
- **Facades and ground floor:** There is no ground floor and there are no shops — nine street-level images and **not one shopfront**. The buildings are set 30–80 m back and mostly hidden behind wall, hedge and tree. When you see one it is either (a) rock-faced random-rubble stone below with cedar shingle above, conical-roofed turrets, four or five massive chimneys and a full-length loggia; (b) all cedar shingle with white-painted trim, a round tower, dormers, a porte-cochère; or (c) a later white modernist box with a red tile roof. Windows are white-framed sash in horizontal bands. The important ground-level features are the **drive, the gate piers, and the porte-cochère** — nothing else.
- **Roofline and clutter:** Steep, complex and busy: hipped and gambrel slopes, dormers in rows, conical turret caps, a tower with an ogee cap, and **stone chimneys that are enormous relative to the house — 1.2 m square, standing 2–3 m above the ridge**. Cedar shingle on both wall and roof, weathered to grey-brown with lighter replacement patches. Almost no visible services clutter — this is the district where a rooftop AC unit would be wrong. At the lighthouse the clutter is different and honest: bolted-on conduit, a galvanised handrail, a lattice radio mast on the ridge.
- **Verticality — poles, cables, signs:** Very sparse. There is overhead wire, but it is **one or two spans crossing the road at a great height** rather than a continuous pole line, and the poles are timber and few. I saw **no streetlights at all** on the estate roads in these images. There are almost no signs: a stop sign, one orange BIKE XING diamond on a temporary stand, and yellow hydrants. The verticals that actually matter are natural: the tree trunks, the flagpole with a flag on it, and the lattice mast on the headland.
- **Materials and their ageing:** Dry-laid fieldstone in the walls — rounded and sub-angular glacial cobbles in grey, grey-brown and rust, no mortar, with the top course slightly loose and moss/lichen on the north faces. Granite and schist bedrock, grey-green, foliated, splitting into big angular steps. Cedar shingle going from tan to dark grey-brown, unevenly, with **visible lighter patches where it has been replaced** and a black bloom in the shaded courses. White trim paint always the freshest and always blistered on the seaward face. Render on the light station white but **rust-streaked from every screw and bracket**, and the red roof shingle faded and lifting at the edges.
- **Colour:** Two palettes that could not be less alike. Newport in leaf: **deep saturated summer greens** — mown lawn, dark clipped hedge, maple canopy — against **grey-brown fieldstone**, weathered **grey-brown shingle**, **brilliant white trim**, and one **red tile roof**. Marin: **dry olive-brown and straw grass over orange-brown soil**, **cream-buff and dark red-brown banded rock**, **international orange** on the bridge, and a blue-grey hazed city. In fog Newport collapses to grey-green with a single red buoy as the only colour in the frame.
- **Light:** The most distinctive thing here is fog, and it is worth building for. Two of the Newport street images are in **sea fog** — no shadows, no sky detail, visibility a few hundred metres, everything desaturated, oncoming cars with their lights on at midday, and the sea wall as the horizon line. The other Newport images are **flat white overcast under a heavy canopy**, so the road is in dappled shade with a blown-out sky. The Marin images are the opposite: raking late light across a dry hillside with long shadows and strong aerial haze separating the near hill from the far city. There are no streetlights, so at night this district is genuinely dark — headlights and house windows only.
- **Vegetation:** Newport: **sugar/red maple and horse chestnut forming a closed canopy over the road**, clipped privet and beech hedges 2.5–3 m tall, Eastern red cedar and juniper massed against the houses, mown lawn everywhere, and **phragmites at the tidal shore**. In late winter the same road is bare grey branches. Marin: no trees at all on the exposed slopes — **coastal scrub and dry bunch grass, olive-green in spring, straw-brown in summer**, with low salt-burnt coyote brush on the seaward faces. The contrast between a fully wooded estate side and a treeless headland side is a real and useful distinction for the district.
- **People and vehicles:** Almost nobody, and the people who are there are **on bicycles** — three of the six Newport images contain cyclists, in ones and small groups, in hi-vis. Vehicles: a silver SUV, a saloon, and nothing else; no parked cars anywhere along the wall because there is no kerb and nowhere to stop. At the lighthouse, two walkers on the footbridge. Cormorants and gulls on the rock.
- **The tells:**
  - No footway, no streetlight, no parked cars — the absence is the signature.
  - Loose granite boulders and whitewashed rocks used as verge edging.
  - The fieldstone wall stopping for a drive and resuming, top course visibly re-laid.
  - A yellow hydrant standing on its own in mown grass with nothing near it.
  - A single overhead wire crossing very high above the canopy.
  - A patch of newer, lighter cedar shingle in an old grey-brown wall.
  - Rust streaks running down white render from every bolt.
  - Black conduit stapled in a straight run across a historic wall.
  - Grass growing up between riprap blocks.
  - A temporary orange BIKE XING sign left standing on the verge.
  - Cyclists in hi-vis as the only people in shot.
  - Fog with car headlights on at midday.

**Where the photographs contradicted what I would have assumed:**
1. **There is no pavement and there are no streetlights.** I would have drawn both. The estate road is carriageway → grass verge → wall, full stop, and at night it is dark. That single fact defines how the district feels to walk or drive.
2. The walls are **dry-laid rounded glacial fieldstone**, not neat coursed dry-stone. They read as a heap of cobbles held in a wall shape, and the top course is uneven.
3. The headland rock is **finely banded and folded chert with red-brown shale partings**, not homogeneous grey cliff. That is why it reads orange at distance and why a plain grey cliff would look wrong.
4. **Fog is a normal condition, not a special effect.** Two of six random street images at the Newport anchor were in fog thick enough to hide the far shore. North Point should have a fog state.
5. The big houses are **stone at the base and shingle above**, and their chimneys are far bigger than intuition suggests.
6. The scenic switchback is **lined bumper-to-bumper with parked cars on the outside shoulder**, with people walking between them and the traffic. I would have built an empty scenic road with a tidy lay-by. The real one is a linear car park with a view.
7. All the buildings on the headland sit **in the valley behind the ridge, never on the ridge line** — only a small concrete lookout hut is on the cliff edge. The skyline stays clean.

## Numbers I could measure or infer
Estimates, scaled off cars (4.7 m), hydrants (0.75 m), people and bicycles.

- **Estate road:** **7.0–8.0 m kerb to kerb**, two lanes, no parking; grass verge 1.5–3.0 m each side; wall at the back of the verge. Total corridor wall to wall 11–14 m.
- **Fieldstone wall:** **0.85–1.15 m high**, 0.5–0.7 m thick, individual stones 150–400 mm, no mortar, coping course uneven by ±80 mm.
- **Hedge:** 2.5–3.2 m tall, clipped flat-topped, 1.0–1.5 m thick.
- **Canopy:** maples 15–20 m tall with the crowns closing over a 7 m road, so the clear height under the canopy is about 5–6 m at the edges.
- **Pleasure-road curve:** radii in the 60–120 m range with visible superelevation of maybe 4–6 %; edge line set 0.3 m in from the pavement edge.
- **Switchback road (Marin):** carriageway **6.5–7.0 m** for two lanes, plus a **2.2–2.6 m outside shoulder used for parking**; yellow kerb-and-channel 0.4 m wide on the uphill side; rock cut rising **10–15 m** at roughly 60–70°; marker posts every 15–25 m on the tight bends.
- **Riprap:** blocks 0.5–1.2 m across, laid two to three deep, slope roughly 1:1.5.
- **Big house:** 2½–3 storeys, floor to floor **3.3–3.8 m**, ridge at 14–18 m, plan 30–45 m across the front, footprint on a plot of 0.4–1.5 ha; setback from the road **30–80 m**; drive 4.0–4.5 m wide, curved, gravel or asphalt.
- **Chimney:** 1.0–1.4 m square, 2.0–3.0 m above the ridge; four to five per house.
- **Turret:** 4.5–5.5 m diameter, conical roof at about 55°.
- **Porte-cochère:** clear width 4.2 m, clear height 3.6 m, projecting 6–7 m.
- **Loggia/porch:** 3.0–3.5 m deep, posts at 2.4–3.0 m centres, eaves at 3.6 m.
- **Footbridge (lighthouse):** deck **1.6–1.8 m wide**, span 35–45 m, portal arches 3.5 m tall, two hanger pairs.
- **Cliff:** 40–70 m from water to the top of the headland at the light; the bare eroding faces are at 45–70° and the vegetated slopes 25–35°.
- **Chert banding:** individual chert beds **20–60 mm thick** with 5–20 mm shale partings; fold wavelength on the order of 1–3 m.
