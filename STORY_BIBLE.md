## Premise

Ashmouth was a port. Containerisation took the real freight down the coast to a deepwater
terminal in the eighties and never gave it back, and what is left is four kilometres of wharf,
a rail spur, a customs shed and nobody watching. **A half-empty port with all its machinery
still working is good for exactly one thing**, and everyone in this story knows it.

The Harbour Trust — the quasi-public body that owns the port land and answers to nobody in
particular — is trying to get the Ash Flats rezoned before a state audit lands. Rezoned, the
made ground is worth four hundred million. Not rezoned, it is a liability with a contaminated
soil report. Everything in the three acts is somebody trying to be on the right side of that
paperwork.

**Dania Kell** does not know or care about any of this. She came off a bulk carrier looking for
her brother.

## Act structure

**ACT ONE — nothing.** Dan pays off the ship at Pier 9 with a duffel and no papers. Tomas has
not answered in five months. She works whatever the Bar will give her — Sonder Bray runs errands
out of a motel office on Tern Bar and asks no questions — and she is good at it, because a marine
engineer can start anything with an engine and fix anything without one. She finds Tomas. He is
in deeper than he said.
**BETRAYAL #1:** Sonder gives her to the Harbour Division to clear his own debt. She loses the
money, the room, and the four months she thought she had.

**ACT TWO — the port.** The union hall takes her in because Rhoda Sandrell needs an engineer who
is not on a list, and because her father **Ilo** decides she is worth teaching. Dan learns the
wharf: what moves at night, whose name is on which manifest, why the crane cab is the best view
in Ashmouth. She rises. Halfway through she has to choose between the Sandrells and Detective
Abasi, who is right about everything and will burn Dan to prove it.
**BETRAYAL #2:** Ilo has been feeding Vessel Halloway for eleven years — it is how Local 9 kept
its berth. When the audit closes in, he gives up Tomas to stay clean. The mentor is the leak.

**ACT THREE — the reckoning.** Dan has nothing left to protect but one person, and the only
lever on Halloway is the thing he needs most: the rezoning file. Three prep beats, then the
finale on the Narrows Bridge and the water under it.
**Ending: escape with cost.** Dan and Tomas get out. Ashmouth does not get better; the Flats get
rezoned anyway, under a different name. The last shot is the city from the water, at dawn,
getting smaller — and the crane still working.

### The five-line test

Each character gets five sample lines in their own file, name removed. If a stranger cannot
identify the speaker, they are the same character twice and one of them gets redesigned.

### Performance — what makes them recognisable before the subtitle

- **Dan** never stands square to anyone; she stands as if there is a bulkhead behind her.
  Cleans her hands on a rag she does not have any more. When she lies, she works something small
  in her fingers.
- **Ilo** leans. On the crane leg, on a bollard, on the doorframe of the union hall. He never
  stands unsupported and he never sits with his back to the water.
- **Halloway** does not turn round when you come in. The room is arranged so you cross it.
- **Sonder** stands too close and touches your elbow.
- **Abasi** puts something between you — a counter, a car door, a table — every single time.
- **Marnie** stays behind the counter. When she comes out from behind it, the scene is serious.
- **Kes** cannot stop moving and will not look at open water for longer than a second.

### Costume as chronology

Dan arrives in shipboard coveralls and steel toecaps — the wrong clothes for a city — and dresses
better as she rises, but never quite right; the boots stay. **Wear accumulates**: dock silt after
Act 1, a dressing on the left forearm that persists two missions after the Kilnward shootout,
Tomas's jacket from the midpoint onward. Ilo's suit gets worse as things get better for him.
If nothing about a character's look ever changes, the story is not touching them.

### Memory

Every character's dialogue changes after: Betrayal #1, Tomas's disappearance, Betrayal #2, and the
player reaching four stars. Costa greets you differently once you are wanted. Marnie's shop
shutters when the story takes her, the radio reports it, and her marker leaves the map. **Anyone
who repeats their intro line after the story has moved reads as a vending machine.**

## Radio

**92.4 Ashmouth Sound** — the only station in the fiction with a live voice. *Late* Teel's night
show, invented ads, a stinger, and **news items tagged to completed missions**, which is the
cheapest narrative in the genre and the highest return. Scripts in `design/world/radio-scripts.md`.

## Brands — invented, and the only ones that may appear on signage

Cormorant Oil · Ashline Freight · Tidewater Marine · Brekker's (corner shops) · Pallas Aggregates
(the quarry) · Sarn Head Lager · Vantage Trust Bank · Ferrow Marine Hire · Meridian Coast
(developer) · Nine Mile Cab · Kell is not a brand, it is a family.

**No real company's mark, name, slogan or livery appears anywhere in Ashmouth**, and nothing is
taken from any existing game. Satire stays in the margins — radio ads, billboards, graffiti. The
A-plot plays straight.
